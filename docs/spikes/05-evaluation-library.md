# Spike 05: Evaluation library — RAGAS or DeepEval (literature review)

**Project:** Traffic rules and penalties assistant · **Team:** Chunky · **Date:** 30/10/2026 · **Time box:** 3 hours (reading only) · **Decision served:** [AD-12 Evaluation](../../Markdown%20Template/11-system-architecture-design-document-local.md#ad-12-evaluation--fully-automated-scored-against-the-d2-answer-key) ("RAGAS or DeepEval, chosen at the first run") · **Jira:** [CHUNKY-128](https://chunky-502.atlassian.net/browse/CHUNKY-128) "EN-08 Evaluate the RAG pipeline against the long-context baseline" (first run that needs the library) · **Prepared by:** Barış Saka

## Question

[AD-12](../../Markdown%20Template/11-system-architecture-design-document-local.md#ad-12-evaluation--fully-automated-scored-against-the-d2-answer-key) decides that every measure is computed automatically and leaves one choice open: whether the two judge-based measures — answer correctness against the answer key of D2 (the 40-question evaluation set, [Deliverables](../../Markdown%20Template/03-project-canvas/05-deliverables.md)) and faithfulness to the retrieved articles — are computed with RAGAS or with DeepEval. This note is a literature review: it records what the two libraries' documentation says, measured against the project's own requirements, and states the expected finding. It does not include a hands-on run; that is EN-08's first evaluation run.

## Terms used in this note

- **Judge (LLM-as-a-judge).** A language model that grades another model's answer. The library is not the judge: the library organises the grading (splits answers into claims, asks the questions, adds up the score), the judge is the model it asks, chosen separately. [AD-12](../../Markdown%20Template/11-system-architecture-design-document-local.md#ad-12-evaluation--fully-automated-scored-against-the-d2-answer-key) requires the judge to be a hosted model that is not the generator, so that no model grades its own answers.
- **Reference-based answer correctness.** Comparing the assistant's answer with the answer key written by the team (the "reference" or "expected output"). Both libraries do this by having the judge break the answer and the reference into claims and check which claims match.
  *Worked example.* Question: "What is the penalty for running a red light?" Answer key: "A 2,000 TL administrative fine is imposed and 20 penalty points are recorded on the licence." Assistant: "A 2,000 TL fine is imposed, and the vehicle is taken off the road for 15 days." The judge first splits both texts into one-sentence claims — assistant: (1) fine 2,000 TL; (2) vehicle off the road 15 days; answer key: (A) fine 2,000 TL; (B) 20 penalty points. It then checks each claim against the other side: (1) matches (A) ✓; (2) has no match in the key ✗ (fabricated); (B) has no match in the answer ✗ (missing). Precision = share of the assistant's claims found in the key = 1/2; recall = share of the key's claims found in the answer = 1/2; Ragas' `FactualCorrectness` reports one of these or their F1 (here 0.5). A "match" is a judgment of meaning, not of wording — "fine is imposed" and "administrative fine is applied" count as the same claim — which is why the judge's command of Turkish matters (R4).
- **Faithfulness / groundedness.** The design document calls this measure "groundedness", both libraries call it "faithfulness"; they are the same thing. Whether every claim in the answer is supported by the articles that were given to the model. Measured without a reference; it catches fabricated numbers and articles. It uses the same claim mechanism as the example above, with one difference: the claims are checked against the retrieved articles instead of the answer key, so claim (2) above fails faithfulness too, because no retrieved article says "15 days".
- **Few-shot examples.** Short worked examples inside a judge prompt that show the judge what a good grading looks like. They are written in English in both libraries.
- **D2, D5, D6, D8, D11; M5.** Deliverable and milestone numbers of the project canvas. D2 is the 40-question evaluation set with its answer key (30 in-scope, 10 out-of-scope); D5 the long-context baseline, D6 the RAG baseline, D8 the beta evaluation, D11 the unseen-set run; M5 the "alpha answers the full question set" milestone (18/11/2026), before which the correctness threshold must be fixed. See [Deliverables](../../Markdown%20Template/03-project-canvas/05-deliverables.md) and [Milestones](../../Markdown%20Template/03-project-canvas/02-milestones.md).
- **Reranker and hybrid retrieval.** Retrieval has two stages. The first finds candidates. In the current design this is dense retrieval: the embedding model turns every article and the question into a list of numbers (a vector) that represents its meaning, so that texts with similar meaning get similar vectors; when a question arrives, the vectors of the 638 articles are compared with the question's vector and the 20 closest become the candidates. The second, the reranker, is a separate small model that reads the question with each of the 20 candidates and picks the best 5; in Spike 03 it lifted retrieval from 17/20 to 20/20 but took 80–90 s per question on the CPU. Hybrid retrieval (Spike 04) changes the first stage, not the second: it adds a keyword search (BM25) next to the dense search and merges the two lists, so that articles the small embedding model misses still reach the candidate list. The two therefore attack the same problem at different points: hybrid gets a missed article *into* the candidates, the reranker moves it *to the top* once it is there. If hybrid alone reaches the same hit@5 (Spike 04, open question 3), the reranker can be dropped or given fewer candidates; the design document still includes it.
- **Single-turn / multi-turn.** A single-turn sample is one question, its retrieved articles and one answer. A multi-turn sample is a whole chat with earlier questions and answers; the follow-up questions of D2 (US-03) need this.

## Working assumption

The team works on the laptop scenario: embedding, reranking and answer models run on the host laptop of [AD-02](../../Markdown%20Template/11-system-architecture-design-document-local.md#ad-02-host--the-core-ultra-7-laptop) (Core Ultra 7 255H, 16 GB RAM, Intel Arc 140T integrated GPU; CPU inference is the baseline, Arc acceleration an experiment), and no hosted model is in use.

## What the library has to do, and what it does not

Every sprint the assistant is tested on the 40 questions of D2. The design document measures five things per answer; three are comparisons of numbers or of a fixed sentence and are done by the team's own code, two are comparisons of meaning and need a judge model. The library is used for those two only.

| Measured | How | Who does it |
|---|---|---|
| Was the right article retrieved? (Retrieval: Recall@k, Precision@k, MRR) | Is the answer key's article ID among the retrieved articles? | Team code; article-ID comparison |
| Was the right article cited? (Supported article references) | Does the answer cite at least one article, and is that ID among the key's supporting articles? | Team code |
| Was the out-of-scope question refused? (Out-of-scope refusal) | Did the 10 out-of-scope questions get the refusal sentence; did any in-scope question get it by mistake? | Team code; fixed-sentence comparison |
| **Is the answer correct?** (Correct answers) | Does the answer agree in meaning with the answer key? | **Library**, with a judge model |
| **Is the answer made up?** (Groundedness / faithfulness) | Is every statement in the answer supported by the retrieved articles? | **Library**, with a judge model |

Query guard, cost and response time are also the team's own measurements. The library choice affects none of these; it affects only the last two rows, and their application to follow-up questions.

The split comes from three places in the design document: the "How it is computed" column of the measures table in the Evaluation section (the first three rows are defined as "rule-based" and article-ID comparison, the last two as "RAGAS or DeepEval"; [design document](../../Markdown%20Template/11-system-architecture-design-document-local.md), section 5); [AD-12](../../Markdown%20Template/11-system-architecture-design-document-local.md#ad-12-evaluation--fully-automated-scored-against-the-d2-answer-key)'s decision sentence ("Citation support and refusal are rule-based. Correctness and groundedness use RAGAS or DeepEval"); and the "Scorer and judge" row of the components table ("rule-based checks plus reference-based metrics … with a judge model").

## Findings

| # | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Both libraries are maintained and current.** Ragas is in its 0.3/0.4 series; DeepEval is at 4.x (October 2026). Both run as Python packages installed with `pip`, both default to OpenAI as judge, both let the judge be replaced. | [1], [9], [10] |
| 2 | **Reference-based correctness (R1).** Ragas has it as a built-in metric: `FactualCorrectness` breaks answer and reference into claims and scores precision, recall or F1 between them (the older `AnswerCorrectness` combines claim overlap with semantic similarity). DeepEval has no built-in correctness metric for RAG; its documented way is `GEval` with a plain-language criterion over `ACTUAL_OUTPUT` and `EXPECTED_OUTPUT` — the docs' own example criterion is "Determine whether the actual output is factually correct based on the expected output." G-Eval produces a 0–1 score from the judge's 0–10 rating, and a rubric can fix the score bands. | [1], [2], [6], [7] |
| 3 | **Faithfulness (R2).** Both have it as a built-in metric with the same design: the judge extracts the claims of the answer and checks each against the retrieved context. Ragas: `Faithfulness`; DeepEval: `FaithfulnessMetric`. | [1], [6] |
| 4 | **Custom judge through an OpenAI-compatible endpoint (R3).** Ragas: `llm_factory(model, provider="openai", client=OpenAI(api_key=..., base_url=...))`; the Ollama example in the docs uses exactly this, and any OpenAI-compatible URL works the same way; 100+ other providers via LiteLLM. DeepEval: `deepeval set-local-model --model=... --base-url=...` or a `LocalModel(model, base_url, api_key)` object passed to each metric; the judge's output format defaults to JSON. Both therefore work with any OpenAI-compatible provider (the Ragas docs' own example is Ollama at `http://localhost:11434/v1`; a hosted provider is configured the same way with its URL and key). | [3], [5] |
| 5 | **Turkish (R4).** The judge prompts of both libraries are written in English, with English few-shot examples. Ragas documents a mechanism for this: `metric.<prompt>.adapt(target_language=..., llm=...)` translates the few-shot examples of each prompt into the target language (instructions too, with `adapt_instruction=True`); Faithfulness and FactualCorrectness each have two prompts that must both be adapted. The docs show Hindi, Spanish and German; Turkish is not listed but nothing limits the mechanism to listed languages. DeepEval has no language parameter: an open issue (#2578) reports that the built-in metrics send hardcoded English prompts regardless of the input language and proposes a `language` parameter; the only workaround today is writing G-Eval criteria in the target language. | [4], [8] |
| 6 | **Judge reliability across languages (R4, R8).** A 2025 study of five LLM judges on five tasks in 25 languages found them inconsistent across languages (average Fleiss' κ ≈ 0.3) and worse on low-resource languages; multilingual training and model size did not fix it. The authors conclude that LLM judges are "not yet reliable for evaluating multilingual predictions" and recommend checking agreement. Whichever library is chosen, the judge's scores must be compared with human grading before the threshold of R1 is fixed. | [11] |
| 7 | **Multi-turn (R5).** DeepEval has the richer model: a `ConversationalTestCase` holds a list of turns, an assistant turn can carry its own `retrieval_context`, and `TurnFaithfulnessMetric`, `TurnContextualRecallMetric` and `ConversationalGEval` score the turns. Ragas has `MultiTurnSample` (a list of human and AI messages), but the documented multi-turn metric is `AspectCritic`, a binary pass/fail on a written criterion; no per-turn faithfulness or correctness is documented. For the project's follow-up measure — "correct answers on the follow-up questions, run as sessions" — both libraries can score the final answer as a single-turn sample whose question carries the earlier turns; DeepEval can additionally score faithfulness per turn. | [6], [12] |
| 8 | **Workflow and reporting (R6, R7).** Ragas is dataset-first: `evaluate()` takes a dataset and returns a scored table (`to_pandas()`), which groups by question type and logs to Weights & Biases in a few lines. DeepEval is test-case-first: metrics run per `LLMTestCase`, usually inside `pytest`, with an optional hosted dashboard (Confident AI) that the project does not need. A June 2026 comparison recommends Ragas for dataset-level RAG tuning (comparing retrievers, chunk sizes, models) and DeepEval for CI regression gates and safety tests; many teams use both. Neither has a native Weights & Biases integration; logging is the team's own code either way. | [1], [13] |
| 9 | **Retrieval metrics are duplicated, not needed.** Both libraries also offer context precision / recall / relevancy metrics judged by the LLM. The design document computes Recall@k, Precision@k and MRR rule-based from the answer key's supporting article IDs; those are exact, free and faster, so the libraries' retrieval metrics are not used. | [1], [6]; design document, Evaluation |
| 10 | **Know-how in the team.** Özge has prior hands-on experience with DeepEval and has stated a preference for it; nobody in the team has used Ragas. A library the team already knows costs less to set up and to debug, and the instructor's remarks on 28/10/2026 treat learning effort as real effort that spikes must account for. | Team (Özge, Oct. 2026); instructor feedback 28/10/2026 |

## What this means for the project

1. **The choice is narrow.** The library computes two measures; the rest is the team's code. Both libraries meet R2, R3 and R7. The decision turns on R1, R4 and R5 — and on finding 10.
2. **Both libraries are usable, and the documentation does not settle the choice.** Each is stronger on some requirements:
   - *Ragas:* reference-based correctness as a built-in metric (R1); a documented mechanism for adapting the judge prompts to Turkish (R4); dataset-first output that matches "report by question type, log every run" (R6). Weaker on multi-turn (R5), which the project can cover by scoring the final answer of each session as a single-turn sample — the measure the design document actually asks for.
   - *DeepEval:* per-turn faithfulness and correctness on follow-up sessions (R5); G-Eval as a flexible correctness metric whose criterion the team writes itself (R1); test-case workflow that can gate pull requests in CI; and prior experience in the team (finding 10). Weaker on R4: the built-in metrics send English judge prompts, so Turkish faithfulness must be checked against human grading before it is trusted, and the correctness criterion for G-Eval must be written in Turkish by the team.
3. **[AD-12](../../Markdown%20Template/11-system-architecture-design-document-local.md#ad-12-evaluation--fully-automated-scored-against-the-d2-answer-key) says the library is "chosen at the first run", and this note keeps it there.** The person who runs EN-08 chooses, with the findings above as the record of what each choice costs. Given finding 10, a first run with DeepEval is a reasonable starting point; a first run with Ragas is the lower-code path for R1 and R4. The decision and its reason go into [AD-12](../../Markdown%20Template/11-system-architecture-design-document-local.md#ad-12-evaluation--fully-automated-scored-against-the-d2-answer-key) and the change log.
4. **Whichever is chosen, the first run must do the same three things.** (a) Make the judge grade in Turkish — Ragas: `adapt()` on both prompts of each metric; DeepEval: Turkish G-Eval criteria and a check of `FaithfulnessMetric` on Turkish text. (b) Check the judge against human grading (R8): comparing the library's faithfulness and correctness scores with a human's "correct / not correct" and "fabrication / none" labels for the same answers shows whether the judge catches what a human catches. (c) Fix the correctness threshold of R1 from that comparison, before M5.
5. **Judge model.** The judge is a hosted model that is not the generator ([AD-12](../../Markdown%20Template/11-system-architecture-design-document-local.md#ad-12-evaluation--fully-automated-scored-against-the-d2-answer-key)); which model is a team decision. Its agreement with human grading (item 4b) is the test of whether it is good enough; if it is not, the fallback is manual scoring by the Domain Expert, the option [AD-12](../../Markdown%20Template/11-system-architecture-design-document-local.md#ad-12-evaluation--fully-automated-scored-against-the-d2-answer-key) rejected for scale.
6. **Model and prompt versions.** The judge model name, the library version and the adapted prompt or criterion version are configuration (`config/rag.yaml`) and go into the AI BOM and every Weights & Biases run.

## Open questions

1. In Ragas, does adapting the instructions as well (`adapt_instruction=True`) change the scores compared with adapting only the few-shot examples? (First run, EN-08.)
2. How closely do the library's faithfulness and correctness scores agree with a human's "correct / not correct" labels — in Ragas with prompts adapted to Turkish, in DeepEval with its English prompts? This one comparison settles two things: the value of the correctness threshold (R8; before M5) and whether DeepEval's English judge prompts (open issue #2578) matter for this project. (First run, EN-08.)
3. Which hosted model serves as judge ([AD-12](../../Markdown%20Template/11-system-architecture-design-document-local.md#ad-12-evaluation--fully-automated-scored-against-the-d2-answer-key): a hosted model that is not the generator), and what does a 40-question run take in time and cost with it? (Team decision before the first run; measured in EN-08.)
4. Does the team want per-turn faithfulness on follow-up sessions (which favours DeepEval), or is the final-answer correctness of the design document enough? (Team decision; Sprint 3 scope.)
